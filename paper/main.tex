\begin{abstract}
Post-training quantization is standard practice for reducing the memory and latency footprints of Large Language Models (LLMs). While traditional compression literature evaluates short-form classification or QA benchmarks using single-seed pass@1 accuracy, reasoning LLMs generate long deliberation traces that introduce complex failure dynamics: token length inflation, termination degeneration, calibration drift, and shifted serving economics. In this work, we present a rigorous empirical study evaluating the joint reliability--calibration--cost frontier of quantized reasoning LLMs under a controlled, pinned serving stack (\texttt{vLLM 0.7.0} eager execution on NVIDIA A100-80GB GPUs). We evaluate 20,000 full-length completions across a 40-cell matrix on the \texttt{MATH-500} benchmark ($n=500$, seeds 42--46) spanning two architectures (\texttt{DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B} and \texttt{DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B}) across four precision formats: full-precision BF16, FP8 (via Marlin W8A16 fallback), AWQ-4, and GPTQ-4. Our findings reveal: (1) FP8 achieves complete statistical parity with BF16 across both architectures (Qwen: $94.40\% \pm 1.05\%$ vs $94.00\% \pm 0.55\%$; Llama: $89.52\% \pm 1.01\%$ vs $89.24\% \pm 0.74\%$), with exact paired McNemar tests showing no significant discordance; (2) 4-bit quantization exhibits architecture-dependent resilience---Qwen-7B retains $>93.1\%$ accuracy, while Llama-8B exhibits sensitivity under AWQ-4 ($86.48\% \pm 1.96\%$); (3) Under eager execution with a 32,768-token window, all 40 cells achieve 0 length truncations and 0 repetition loops; and (4) 4-bit quantization introduces a $+3.9\%$ to $+6.5\%$ token inflation penalty, shifting the Cost-of-Pass ($C_{\mathrm{pass}}$) frontier such that FP8 consistently delivers the optimal dollar-cost-per-correct answer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{R}{easoning} Large Language Models (such as DeepSeek-R1) achieve competitive mathematical and scientific reasoning by generating extensive step-by-step intermediate traces before emitting a final solution. However, generating thousands of deliberation tokens per query incurs severe memory pressure and prolonged latency. Consequently, post-training quantization (FP8, AWQ-4, GPTQ-4) has become a prerequisite for real-world serving.

Existing evaluations suffer from three systematic weaknesses: (1) \textit{Accuracy Tunnel-Vision}: pass@1 alone obscures whether compression degrades calibration, selective risk, or consensus stability; (2) \textit{Hidden Token Inflation}: compressed models often require redundant intermediate steps to arrive at a correct answer, inflating token volume and driving up serving costs; and (3) \textit{Software Stack Confounding}: discrepancies in inference engine versions, JIT compilation, and decoding configurations frequently dominate observed compression effects.

To establish causal clarity, this paper isolates the effect of weight quantization on mathematical reasoning under strict serving-stack control. We formulate four core questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Correctness):} Does quantization degrade reasoning accuracy relative to BF16 under matched multi-seed evaluation?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Trace Pathologies):} Does compression induce pathological repetition loops or context-cap truncations?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Calibration Metrology):} How does quantization impact sample-consistency calibration (ECE, Brier, AURC)?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Deployment Economics):} Does token inflation alter the Cost-of-Pass ($C_{\mathrm{pass}}$) Pareto frontier?
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Models and Precision Formats}
We evaluate two distilled reasoning architectures:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \texttt{DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B}
    \item \texttt{DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B}
\end{enumerate}
For each architecture, we benchmark four formats: uncompressed \textbf{BF16}, \textbf{FP8} (executed via Marlin W8A16 weight-only fallback on NVIDIA A100), \textbf{AWQ-4} (group size 128, \texttt{float16}), and \textbf{GPTQ-4} (group size 128, Marlin kernel).

\subsection{Controlled Protocol (Protocol P1-2026-08)}
Evaluations are conducted on \texttt{HuggingFaceH4/MATH-500} ($n=500$). Prompting uses the standard zero-shot DeepSeek template requesting boxed answers (\texttt{\textbackslash boxed\{\}}), initialized with the \texttt{<think>\textbackslash n} token. Sampling parameters are fixed across all runs: temperature $T=0.6$, top-$p=0.95$, repetition penalty $1.0$, and maximum generation length $32,768$ tokens. We evaluate 5 distinct seeds ($42, 43, 44, 45, 46$), yielding $2 \times 4 \times 5 \times 500 = 20,000$ generated completions.

\subsection{Hardware and Serving Stack}
All experiments run on PARAM Rudra HPC using dedicated NVIDIA A100-PCIE-80GB GPUs. The serving stack is pinned to \texttt{vLLM 0.7.0} in eager execution mode (\texttt{--enforce-eager}) with GPU memory utilization set to $0.75$ (reserving 60GB VRAM) to prevent shared contention and eliminate runtime Triton JIT linker faults.

\section{Empirical Results}

\subsection{Accuracy, Stability, and Parity}
As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:headline_results}, FP8 quantization achieves complete statistical parity with the full-precision BF16 baselines across both architectures ($94.40\%$ vs $94.00\%$ on Qwen-7B; $89.52\%$ vs $89.24\%$ on Llama-8B). In 4-bit precision, Qwen-7B exhibits high resilience, maintaining $93.48\%$ under GPTQ-4 and $93.12\%$ under AWQ-4. In contrast, Llama-8B experiences a $2.76\%$ drop under AWQ-4 ($86.48\%$). Across all 20,000 completions, we observed exactly 0 length truncations and 0 infinite repetition loops.

\subsection{Calibration and Uncertainty Metrology}
Table~\ref{tab:calibration} reports Expected Calibration Error (ECE), Brier score, and Area Under the Risk-Coverage Curve (AURC) derived from sample-consistency confidence scores ($\hat{c}_i = \frac{1}{5}\sum_{s=1}^5 \mathbb{I}(\text{correct})$). Sample consistency provides a well-calibrated confidence estimator ($\text{ECE} \le 0.034$ on Qwen-7B, $\text{ECE} \le 0.072$ on Llama-8B), with near-zero selective risk ($\text{AURC} \le 0.0054$).

\subsection{Deployment Economics and Cost-of-Pass}
We quantify serving economics under cloud GPU pricing ($R = \$1.50/\text{A100-Hour}$):
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{pass}} = \frac{\bar{T}_{\mathrm{tokens}} \cdot R}{3600 \cdot \text{Throughput} \cdot \text{Pass@1}}
\end{equation}
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:headline_results}, 4-bit quantization increases mean generation length by $+6.5\%$ on Qwen-7B (4,287 vs 4,011 tokens) and $+3.9\%$ on Llama-8B (4,840 vs 4,656 tokens). Because FP8 avoids token inflation while matching baseline accuracy, \textbf{FP8 defines the Pareto-optimal Cost-of-Pass ($C_{\mathrm{pass}}$)} across both architectures (\$0.0272/correct for Qwen; \$0.0326/correct for Llama).

\section{Threats to Validity \& Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A100 Weight-Only FP8 Fallback:} On NVIDIA A100 GPUs, FP8 checkpoints execute via vLLM's Marlin weight-only fallback kernel (W8A16). Native FP8 tensor-core compute (W8A8) requires Ada Lovelace or Hopper architectures.
    \item \textbf{Task Scope:} Primary headline results are established on \texttt{MATH-500}. Ongoing breadth extensions evaluate expert science reasoning (\texttt{GPQA-Diamond}) and arithmetic reasoning (\texttt{GSM8K}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
This paper establishes the empirical reliability--calibration--cost frontier of quantized reasoning LLMs across 20,000 completions. We demonstrate that FP8 achieves complete statistical parity with BF16 while avoiding token inflation, establishing the optimal Cost-of-Pass deployment boundary.
