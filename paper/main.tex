\begin{abstract}
Post-training quantization is standard practice for reducing the prohibitive memory and latency footprints of Large Language Models (LLMs). While traditional compression literature evaluates short-form classification or QA benchmarks using single-seed pass@1 accuracy, reasoning LLMs generate long deliberation chains that introduce subtle, multi-step failure dynamics: token length inflation, termination anomalies, calibration drift, and shifted serving economics. In this work, we present a rigorous empirical study evaluating the joint reliability--calibration--cost frontier of quantized reasoning LLMs under a controlled, pinned serving stack (\texttt{vLLM 0.7.0} eager execution on NVIDIA A100-80GB GPUs). We evaluate over 56,400 full-length completions across 88 experimental cells spanning mathematical competition reasoning (\texttt{MATH-500}, $n=500$, seeds 42--46), grade-school arithmetic (\texttt{GSM8K}, $n=1,319$, seeds 42--44), and graduate-level scientific reasoning (\texttt{GPQA-Diamond}, $n=198$, seeds 42--44) across two architectures (\texttt{DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B} and \texttt{DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B}) and four precision formats: full-precision BF16, FP8 (via Marlin W8A16 fallback), AWQ-4, and GPTQ-4. 

Our findings demonstrate that: (1) FP8 achieves 100\% statistical parity with BF16 across all tasks (Qwen MATH-500: $94.40\% \pm 1.05\%$ vs $94.00\% \pm 0.55\%$; Llama MATH-500: $89.52\% \pm 1.01\%$ vs $89.24\% \pm 0.74\%$), with exact paired McNemar tests showing no significant discordance under Holm-Bonferroni correction ($p > 0.29$); (2) 4-bit quantization exhibits architecture-dependent resilience---Qwen-7B retains $>93.1\%$ accuracy on MATH-500 with tight calibration error ($\mathrm{ECE} \le 0.034$), whereas Llama-8B exhibits sensitivity under AWQ-4 ($86.48\% \pm 1.96\%$); (3) Across all 88 cells, eager execution with a 32,768-token window achieves exactly 0 length truncations and 0 infinite repetition loops ($>99.5\%$ boxed parse success); (4) 4-bit quantization introduces a $+3.9\%$ to $+6.5\%$ token inflation penalty on borderline problems, shifting the Cost-of-Pass ($C_{\mathrm{pass}}$) Pareto frontier such that FP8 consistently delivers the optimal dollar-cost-per-correct answer; and (5) Sample-consistency filtering ($\ge 5/5$ consensus) increases selective accuracy to $>98.2\%$ while capping selective risk below $1.8\%$. We release all reproducible artifacts, configurations, and raw per-problem validation logs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Reasoning Large Language Models (such as DeepSeek-R1 \cite{rein2023gpqa}) achieve competitive mathematical and scientific reasoning by generating extensive step-by-step intermediate deliberation traces before emitting a final answer. However, generating thousands of autoregressive tokens per query incurs severe GPU memory pressure and prolonged latency. Consequently, post-training weight quantization (e.g., FP8, AWQ-4 \cite{lin2023awq}, GPTQ-4 \cite{frantar2022gptq}) has become a prerequisite for real-world serving.

Despite widespread adoption, current evaluation literature exhibits three fundamental limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Isolated Accuracy Metrics:} Standard studies report pass@1 accuracy in isolation, obscuring whether weight degradation alters sample consistency, selective prediction risk, or uncertainty calibration \cite{guo2017calibration, zollo2026unsupervised}.
    \item \textbf{Hidden Token Inflation \& Cost Shifts:} While compressed models may reach the correct final answer, weight distortion often prompts redundant deliberation sub-steps, inflating output token lengths and elevating the effective \textbf{Cost-of-Pass ($C_{\mathrm{pass}}$)} \cite{chen2025costofpass, zhang2026quantinflates}.
    \item \textbf{Software Stack Confounding:} Discrepancies in serving backends, JIT compilation, and decoding configurations frequently dominate compression effects, leading to conflicting empirical reports in recent literature \cite{sanyal2025sober}.
\end{enumerate}

To establish causal clarity, this paper isolates the effect of weight quantization on mathematical and scientific reasoning under strict serving-stack control. We formulate four core Research Questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Correctness \& Stability):} Does quantization degrade reasoning accuracy relative to full-precision BF16 across multi-seed evaluations?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Trace Integrity \& Pathologies):} Does compression trigger pathological failure modes, such as infinite repetition loops or context-cap truncations?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Calibration \& Selective Risk):} How does quantization affect sample-consistency confidence, Expected Calibration Error (ECE), and selective risk (AURC)?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Deployment Economics):} Does token inflation alter the dollar-cost-per-correct answer ($C_{\mathrm{pass}}$) on datacenter hardware?
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Methodology}
\subsection{Models and Precision Formats}
We evaluate two widely deployed reasoning architectures:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \texttt{DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B} (Qwen2.5 backbone, 151k vocabulary)
    \item \texttt{DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B} (Llama-3.1 backbone, 128k vocabulary)
\end{enumerate}
For each architecture, four precision formats are evaluated: uncompressed \textbf{BF16} (\texttt{torch.bfloat16}), \textbf{FP8} (executed via vLLM's Marlin W8A16 weight-only fallback kernel on NVIDIA A100), \textbf{AWQ-4} (group size 128, \texttt{torch.float16}), and \textbf{GPTQ-4} (group size 128, Marlin kernel).

\subsection{Evaluation Benchmarks and Protocol}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MATH-500 ($n=500$ \cite{hendrycks2021math}):} Canonical competition mathematics benchmark across 7 subjects and 5 difficulty tiers, evaluated across 5 seeds ($42, 43, 44, 45, 46$) totaling 20,000 completions.
    \item \textbf{GSM8K ($n=1,319$ \cite{cobbe2021gsm8k}):} Multi-step grade-school arithmetic word problems, evaluated across seeds $42, 43, 44$ (31,656 completions).
    \item \textbf{GPQA-Diamond ($n=198$ \cite{rein2023gpqa}):} Graduate-level scientific reasoning MCQs, evaluated across seeds $42, 43, 44$ (4,752 completions).
    \item \textbf{Decoding Parameters:} Zero-shot prompting with \texttt{<think>\textbackslash n} assistant prefix, temperature $T=0.6$, top-$p=0.95$, repetition penalty $1.0$, and max token window $32,768$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hardware and Serving Stack}
All experiments were conducted on PARAM Rudra HPC (IIT Patna / NSM) using dedicated NVIDIA A100-PCIE-80GB GPUs (16 host CPUs per task). The serving engine is strictly pinned to \texttt{vLLM==0.7.0} with \texttt{--enforce-eager} and \texttt{--gpu-memory-utilization 0.75} (reserving 60GB VRAM) to prevent shared contention.

\subsection{Mathematical Formulations of Evaluation Metrics}
\subsubsection{Sample-Consistency Confidence and Calibration}
For problem $x_i$, let $y_{i,s}$ denote the answer from seed $s \in \{1,\dots,K\}$. The majority consensus answer is $\hat{y}_i = \mathrm{mode}(\{y_{i,1},\dots,y_{i,K}\})$, with empirical confidence:
\begin{equation}
\hat{c}_i = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{s=1}^K \mathbb{I}(y_{i,s} = \hat{y}_i)
\end{equation}
Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Brier Score (BS) over $M$ confidence bins $B_m$ are defined as:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ECE} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{|B_m|}{N} \left| \mathrm{acc}(B_m) - \mathrm{conf}(B_m) \right|, \quad \mathrm{BS} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N (\hat{c}_i - z_i)^2
\end{equation}
where $z_i \in \{0,1\}$ is the correctness indicator of $\hat{y}_i$.

\subsubsection{Area Under Risk-Coverage Curve (AURC)}
Let $\kappa \in [0, 1]$ denote the sample coverage rate when rejecting answers below confidence threshold $\gamma$. Selective risk is $\mathrm{Risk}(\kappa) = \frac{1}{|S_\kappa|} \sum_{i \in S_\kappa} (1 - z_i)$. The Area Under the Risk-Coverage Curve is:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AURC} = \int_0^1 \mathrm{Risk}(\kappa) \, d\kappa
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Cost-of-Pass ($C_{\mathrm{pass}}$) Formulation}
Given cloud GPU rental rate $R = \$1.50/\mathrm{GPU\text{-}Hour}$ ($\$0.0004167/\mathrm{GPU\text{-}sec}$) and generation throughput $\tau = 65\text{ tok/s}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Cost\text{ }per\text{ }Query} = \frac{\bar{T}_{\mathrm{tokens}}}{\tau} \times \frac{R}{3600}, \quad C_{\mathrm{pass}} = \frac{\mathrm{Cost\text{ }per\text{ }Query}}{\mathrm{Pass@1}}
\end{equation}

\section{Threats to Validity \& Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A100 W8A16 Fallback:} On NVIDIA A100 GPUs, FP8 checkpoints execute via vLLM's Marlin weight-only fallback kernel (W8A16). Native FP8 W8A8 compute requires Ada Lovelace or Hopper architectures.
    \item \textbf{Serving Stack Specificity:} All findings were established under pinned \texttt{vLLM 0.7.0} eager mode. Experimental v1 schedulers may alter preemption overheads.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
This paper establishes the empirical reliability--calibration--cost frontier of quantized reasoning models across 56,400+ completions. FP8 achieves universal statistical parity with BF16, while 4-bit compression induces quantifiable token inflation. Sample-consistency confidence provides robust uncertainty calibration, and FP8 defines the Pareto-optimal Cost-of-Pass deployment boundary.
