\begin{abstract}
Post-training quantization is a common serving default for reasoning language models, but pass@1 on a single seed can hide changes in trace length, termination behavior, and sample-level reliability. We pin one serving stack---vLLM~0.7.0~\cite{kwon2023vllm} eager execution on an NVIDIA A100-80GB, with FP8 checkpoints executed as Marlin W8A16~\cite{frantar2024marlin} rather than native W8A8---and vary only the weight checkpoint among BF16, FP8, AWQ-4, and GPTQ-4 for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B. The grid has 88 cells and 56{,}408 completions on MATH-500~\cite{hendrycks2021math,lightman2023lets} (5 seeds), GSM8K~\cite{cobbe2021gsm8k} (3 seeds), and GPQA-Diamond~\cite{rein2023gpqa} (3 seeds).

On MATH-500, FP8--BF16 pass@1 differences are $+0.40$ and $+0.28$ percentage points (pp). Problem-clustered bootstrap 95\% intervals include zero; a $\pm 1$\,pp TOST equivalence test is \emph{not} passed. Four-bit effects are architecture- and task-dependent: Llama AWQ-4 loses $2.76$\,pp on MATH-500 ($95\%$ CI $[-4.16,-1.44]$, $p<0.001$) and $1.57$\,pp on GSM8K; Qwen AWQ-4 loses $5.56$\,pp on GPQA-Diamond ($95\%$ CI $[-9.60,-1.52]$, $p=0.007$). Majority-vote McNemar tests on maj@5 remain non-significant and do not answer the pass@1 question. Mean MATH-500 length rises $6.3$--$6.8\%$ for Qwen 4-bit (paired token CIs exclude zero); the increase is concentrated on items where BF16 is correct and the quantized run is not. Identical-word loops are rare but nonzero ($25/56{,}408$); none of the rows hit the $32{,}768$ token cap after re-encoding, yet $209$ completions sit at $\ge 32{,}500$ tokens, and Qwen 4-bit near-cap counts on MATH-500 are about $1.7\times$ BF16. Using recovered answer strings, gold-free modal agreement is an observable abstention signal: 5/5 consensus has very low selective error on MATH-500 but costs five generations per query, and Llama AWQ-4 reduces 5/5 coverage by $6.0$\,pp versus BF16 ($95\%$ paired CI $[-9.4,-2.6]$). On a frozen 100-problem MATH-500 serving subset, batched Qwen-7B FP8 decode is $+18.7\%$ faster than matched BF16 ($824$ vs.\ $694$ tok/s) and $-19.8\%$ lower in measured Cost-of-Pass at a $\$1.50$/A100-hour scenario. Four-bit throughput is concurrency-dependent: every 4-bit cell is slower than BF16 in single-stream decode, while batched Llama GPTQ-4 reaches $786$ tok/s. Peak allocated VRAM stays $\sim 55$--$57$\,GB across formats under \texttt{gpu\_memory\_utilization=0.75} and does not measure weight-footprint savings.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{0.6em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Reasoning language models, post-training quantization, pinned serving stack, token inflation, selective prediction, Cost-of-Pass.

\section{Introduction}
Reasoning models such as DeepSeek-R1~\cite{guo2025deepseekr1} emit long traces before a final answer. Weight-only post-training quantization (FP8, AWQ-4~\cite{lin2023awq}, GPTQ-4~\cite{frantar2022gptq}) is therefore a common serving choice. Isolated pass@1 numbers hide whether compression also changes trace length~\cite{lian2026quantinflates,liu2025qrm}, termination, or the cost of a correct answer~\cite{erol2025costofpass}. Serving-stack details---engine version, decoding flags, CUDA graphs---can move reasoning scores as much as model changes~\cite{sanyal2025sober}. This paper \emph{pins} one stack rather than shifting it: we do not run a factorial vLLM $0.7.0$ vs.\ $0.8.5$ experiment.

We ask four questions under that pin:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1.} Does quantization change \emph{pass@1} relative to matched BF16, with problem-clustered uncertainty?
    \item \textbf{RQ2.} Do identical-word loops and near-budget terminations occur, and do they differ by format?
    \item \textbf{RQ3.} What can be said about multi-sample reliability without gold labels at serve time?
    \item \textbf{RQ4.} How do measured decode throughput and GPU-seconds per query change Cost-of-Pass relative to a shared-throughput token proxy?
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
(1)~A released, format-matched, stack-pinned 88-cell grid (56{,}408 completions) with per-cell JSON.
(2)~Problem-clustered pass@1 inference showing FP8--BF16 intervals that include zero, a statistically detectable Llama AWQ-4 drop on MATH-500/GSM8K, and a Qwen AWQ-4 drop on GPQA-Diamond---effects that maj@5 McNemar tests miss.
(3)~Evidence that 4-bit token inflation is real for Qwen on the full MATH-500 grid, is \emph{not} an artifact of a 200-item even-index subset, and is concentrated on format-induced failures rather than uniformly longer correct traces.
(4)~Gold-free modal-answer agreement on MATH-500: higher observable agreement is associated with lower selective error, while 4-bit AWQ reduces strict-consensus coverage in some configurations even when selective error among retained predictions stays low.
(5)~Measured serving on the same pinned stack (48 task-realistic runs $+$ 8 fixed-token microbenchmarks): Cost-of-Pass uses campaign MATH-500 pass@1 and measured GPU-seconds rather than a shared $65$ tok/s assumption.

\section{Related Work}
GPTQ~\cite{frantar2022gptq} and AWQ~\cite{lin2023awq} were originally validated mainly on short-form benchmarks; SmoothQuant~\cite{xiao2023smoothquant} addresses activation quantization, which we do not run. Liu et al.~\cite{liu2025qrm} evaluate DeepSeek-R1 distillations across weight, activation, and KV quantization and report near-lossless W8/W4A16 in many settings, with \emph{no} systematic output lengthening. Lian et al.~\cite{lian2026quantinflates} report the opposite: low-bit PTQ can inflate chain-of-thought length. Li et al.~\cite{li2025quantmeets} localize 4-bit math failures to early process errors. Alimaskina et al.~\cite{alimaskina2026extremelowbit} study loops and delayed commitment under extreme low-bit inference. Our delta is a single pinned vLLM~0.7.0 eager path~\cite{kwon2023vllm} with five MATH-500 seeds and paired item-level tests, not a new compressor.

Hochlehnert et al.~\cite{sanyal2025sober} show that seeds, prompts, decoding, and software move reasoning scores; we freeze those factors. Zhong et al.~\cite{zhong2025quantized} study uncertainty calibration of quantized LLMs using model probabilities---a quantity we do not log. Self-consistency~\cite{wang2022selfconsistency} and later unsupervised estimators~\cite{zollo2026unsupervised} treat sample agreement as confidence; selective classification~\cite{geifman2017selective} supplies the risk--coverage language. Erol et al.~\cite{erol2025costofpass} define Cost-of-Pass as expected monetary cost per correct solution; we report it twice: a historical shared-$65$ tok/s token proxy, and a measured GPU-second version on a frozen MATH-500 serving subset.

\section{Experimental Methodology}
\subsection{Models and precision formats}
We evaluate DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B~\cite{guo2025deepseekr1}. Formats: uncompressed \textbf{BF16}; \textbf{FP8} checkpoints served on A100 via vLLM's Marlin \textbf{W8A16} weight-only fallback (not native W8A8 tensor-core FP8); \textbf{AWQ-4} (group size 128, \texttt{float16}); \textbf{GPTQ-4} (group size 128, Marlin). Hugging Face IDs and revisions are listed in the appendix. AWQ-4 checkpoints are third-party community uploads; ``Llama AWQ-4 is weak'' is a property of those artifacts, not a proof about AWQ as a method.

\subsection{Benchmarks and protocol}
MATH-500~\cite{hendrycks2021math,lightman2023lets} ($n=500$, seeds $42$--$46$): 20{,}000 completions. GSM8K~\cite{cobbe2021gsm8k} ($n=1{,}319$, seeds $42$--$44$) and GPQA-Diamond~\cite{rein2023gpqa} ($n=198$, seeds $42$--$44$): breadth. Decoding: zero-shot \texttt{<think>} prefix (appendix), $T=0.6$, top-$p=0.95$, repetition penalty $1.0$ (deliberately not used to hide loops), max new tokens $32{,}768$. Total: $56{,}408$ completions in 88 cells. MATH and GSM8K are widely used; contamination likely inflates absolute scores but largely cancels in within-model format contrasts.

\subsection{Hardware and serving stack}
PARAM Rudra HPC (IIT Patna / NSM), NVIDIA A100-PCIE-80GB. Publication runs use the official QRM \texttt{inference.py} path~\cite{liu2025qrm} with \texttt{gpu\_memory\_utilization=0.75}. Engine pin: \texttt{vLLM==0.7.0}, eager execution. Source files under \texttt{configs/models/} are \emph{not} the campaign launcher; they differ in \texttt{max\_model\_len} and KV-cache defaults and should not be read as the effective stack.

\subsection{Measured serving}
\label{sec:measured-serving}
After the 56k accuracy campaign we timed the same eight checkpoints on A100-80GB under vLLM~0.7.0 eager (protocol: \texttt{docs/MEASURED\_SERVING\_PROTOCOL.md}). Workload: a frozen 100-problem MATH-500 list (20 problems per difficulty label). Prompts are stored as \texttt{full\_prompt} in \texttt{results/measured\_serving/input\_subset.json}; the sidecar \texttt{problem}/\texttt{solution} metadata in that JSON is misaligned with the prompts and is not used. Condition~A: sequential single-request decode of the first 20 prompts of that list (level counts $5/7/3/3/2$, not a re-stratified 4-per-level draw), $R=3$. Condition~B: one \texttt{llm.generate} call over all 100 prompts (continuous batching; labeled $C=8$ in the protocol, but \texttt{max\_num\_seqs} was not pinned to 8). Secondary microbenchmark: 10 prompts forced to exactly 512 tokens (\texttt{ignore\_eos}). Three warmup queries are discarded. Sampling matches the campaign ($T=0.6$, top-$p=0.95$) with seed $20260816$. Repetitions share that seed, so output token counts are identical across reps; the $R=3$ spread is wall-clock noise, not generation variability. Jobs ran on \texttt{ragpu003}/\texttt{ragpu004}/\texttt{ragpu008} (PyTorch 2.5.1+cu124). Llama AWQ-4 and Llama GPTQ-4 have records from more than one hostname.

Output tok/s $=(\sum \text{output tokens})/\Delta t_{\mathrm{steady}}$. GPU-sec/query $=\Delta t_{\mathrm{steady}}/N$ on one GPU. Peak VRAM is \texttt{torch.cuda.max\_memory\_allocated} after the $0.75$ utilization preallocation ($\sim 60$\,GB reserved of 80\,GB). Measured
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{pass}}^{\mathrm{meas}} = \frac{(\text{GPU-sec/query})\times (\$1.50/3600)}{\mathrm{pass@1}},
\end{equation}
using the frozen 40-cell MATH-500 means---not accuracy on the 100-item subset. The $\$1.50$/A100-hour rate is a cloud pricing \emph{scenario}, not a billed cluster price.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
\paragraph{Pass@1.}
Mean extractive match across seeds. Primary inference is a problem-clustered bootstrap of the paired difference $\Delta = \overline{\mathrm{acc}}_{\mathrm{quant}} - \overline{\mathrm{acc}}_{\mathrm{BF16}}$ (resample $n$ items with replacement; retain all seeds per item; $B=10{,}000$). We report $\Delta$ in percentage points, a 95\% percentile CI, and a two-sided bootstrap $p$-value, with Holm--Bonferroni across the six MATH (resp.\ GSM8K, GPQA) contrasts. A $\pm 1$\,pp two one-sided tests (TOST) using the 90\% CI is reported as an equivalence check, not as a default claim.

\paragraph{maj@5 McNemar (secondary).}
A problem is maj@5-correct if at least 3 of 5 MATH-500 seeds match gold. Exact McNemar tests this consensus, not pass@1.

\paragraph{Pathology.}
A row is a \emph{loop} if the longest consecutive identical-word run is $\ge 20$ (Unicode word tokens, case-folded). A row is a \emph{cap hit} if re-encoded completion length is $\ge 32{,}768$. Because decode-then-re-tokenize undershoots the cap (observed max $32{,}737$), we also count \emph{near-cap} rows with $\ge 32{,}500$ tokens. Saved rows do not include vLLM \texttt{finish\_reason} or output token IDs; phrase-level cycles are invisible to the identical-word detector.

\paragraph{Length.}
Primary estimator: ratio of means over all seeds, plus the mean paired per-item token delta with a clustered bootstrap CI. We also report the mean of per-item ratios, which is a different estimand and is dominated by short BF16 traces. Length is stratified by the $2\times 2$ of BF16/quantized extractive correctness.

\paragraph{Historical token-implied $C_{\mathrm{pass}}$.}
Following Erol et al.~\cite{erol2025costofpass}, $C_{\mathrm{pass}} = (\text{cost per query}) / \text{pass@1}$ with
\begin{equation}
\text{Cost per query} = (\bar{T}/\tau)\times (R/3600),
\end{equation}
$\tau = 65$ tok/s (unmeasured) and $R=\$1.50$ per A100-hour. Because $\tau$ and $R$ are shared, ranking by $C_{\mathrm{pass}}$ is ranking by $\bar{T}/\mathrm{pass@1}$. We retain this proxy only as a sensitivity to the pre-measurement draft; primary systems/cost evidence is the measured GPU-second version in Section~\ref{sec:measured-serving} and Table~\ref{tab:serving-batched}.

\paragraph{Observable modal agreement.}
\label{sec:modal-def}
Compact per-cell JSON stores \texttt{extractive\_match} but not extracted strings. After recovering the 20{,}000 MATH-500 answer strings, we form agreement \emph{without} gold. Campaign extraction and clustering used LightEval~0.8.0 with math-verify (frozen in \texttt{docs/ANSWER\_NORMALIZATION.md}). A later throwaway MacBook install of LightEval~0.8.1 was \emph{not} used for canonical numbers. For item $i$ and five generated predictions, let $\sim$ be that campaign mathematical-equivalence relation. Cluster the five predictions into connected components of $\sim$. Let $m_i$ be the size of the unique largest class when one exists; otherwise the item is a non-unique-mode tie. Observable agreement is $a_i=m_i/5$. Serve at threshold $k/5$ only when a unique modal class satisfies $m_i\ge k$. The pattern $(A,A,B,B,C)$ has $m_i=2$ and is not served at $\ge 3/5$; $(A,A,A,B,B)$ has unique mode $A$ with $m_i=3$ and is served at $\ge 3/5$. Gold is used only afterward to estimate selective accuracy and risk. All five samples are generated before the decision. The five-sample token-cost proxy $T_5$ is the sum of their completion tokens, including abstained queries. We report three discrete operating points, not a continuous risk--coverage curve. Gold-hit ECE (confidence $=c_i/5$, label $=\mathbb{I}(c_i\ge 3)$) remains a deterministic function of the gold-hit histogram and is omitted.

\section{Results}
\subsection{MATH-500 pass@1}
Table~\ref{tab:headline_results} and Figure~\ref{fig:seed} show per-seed pass@1. FP8 point estimates sit $0.28$--$0.40$\,pp above BF16. Llama AWQ-4 is the weak MATH cell ($-2.76$\,pp).

Table~\ref{tab:pass1} is the test that matches RQ1. Llama AWQ-4's clustered interval excludes zero ($p<0.001$; Holm-significant among the six MATH contrasts). Qwen 4-bit MATH drops are negative but not Holm-significant. No MATH contrast, including FP8, has its 90\% CI inside $[-1,+1]$\,pp, so we do \emph{not} claim statistical equivalence at a $1$\,pp serving margin. Secondary maj@5 McNemar $p$-values are all $>0.29$: majority voting absorbs the per-seed flips that pass@1 still sees.

\subsection{GSM8K and GPQA-Diamond}
Table~\ref{tab:multitask}: FP8 stays within about one point of BF16 on all three tasks. The largest accuracy effect in the study is Qwen AWQ-4 on GPQA-Diamond ($-5.56$\,pp, $95\%$ CI $[-9.60,-1.52]$, $p=0.007$, Holm-significant among six GPQA contrasts; 3 seeds, $n=198$). Other GSM8K Qwen contrasts pass TOST at $\pm 1$\,pp. GPQA intervals are wide; we treat non-AWQ GPQA contrasts as exploratory.

\subsection{Loops and near-cap termination}
\label{sec:pathology}
Across 88 cells the validator records \textbf{25} repetition-flagged rows ($0.044\%$ of 56{,}408) and \textbf{0} exact cap hits. Earlier drafts reported $0/0$ because analysis scripts read \texttt{truncation\_count} / \texttt{repetition\_flag\_count} while the validator writes \texttt{token\_limit\_hits} / \texttt{repetition\_rows}. Loops occur in BF16 as well as quantized cells (Llama MATH: 3/3/3/2 for BF16/FP8/AWQ/GPTQ). Three runs exceed 10{,}000 identical consecutive words. Near-cap rows ($\ge 32{,}500$ tokens) total 209; on MATH-500, Qwen AWQ-4/GPTQ-4 have 25 and 24 versus BF16's 14, and almost all near-cap rows are unboxed. Without \texttt{finish\_reason} these are inferred length terminations, not proven \texttt{length} stops.

\subsection{Token length}
\label{sec:economics}
On the \emph{full} MATH-500 grid and all five seeds (Table~\ref{tab:tokens}, Figure~\ref{fig:tokens}), Qwen AWQ-4/GPTQ-4 generate $+6.33\%$/$+6.88\%$ more tokens than BF16 (mean paired $\Delta$ $+254$ and $+276$ tokens; CIs exclude zero). Llama GPTQ-4 is $+3.95\%$ ($+184$ tokens, CI just excludes zero). Llama AWQ-4 $+1.72\%$ and both FP8 cells have CIs that include zero.

A previous 200-item ``stratified mixed-correctness'' analysis used even indices among the first 400 problems, seed 42 only, and the \emph{mean of per-item ratios}. That estimator is not the full-grid ratio of means: on the same 200 items it reports Qwen FP8 $+11.4\%$ while the ratio of means is $-3.4\%$. We discard that subset interpretation.

Figure~\ref{fig:strata} and Table~\ref{tab:tokens} split paired token deltas by correctness. When both formats are correct, 4-bit adds on the order of $+90$ to $+170$ tokens. When BF16 is correct and the quantized sample is wrong, the quantized trace is thousands of tokens longer. Token inflation is therefore largely a \emph{failure-associated} lengthening, which is a more useful reconciliation of Liu et al.~\cite{liu2025qrm} (little mean lengthening) and Lian et al.~\cite{lian2026quantinflates} (hidden token cost) than a 200-item even-index slice.

\subsection{Observable modal agreement on MATH-500}
\label{sec:modal}
Recovered answer strings make RQ3 measurable as observable multi-sample agreement, selective prediction, and abstention---not as model safety or softmax calibration. Table~\ref{tab:modal} reports coverage and selective risk at three discrete thresholds. On MATH-500, higher observable answer agreement is associated with lower selective error. At 5/5 consensus, selective error is at most $0.27\%$ across the evaluated configurations, but coverage ranges from $70.2\%$ (Llama AWQ-4) to $88.8\%$ (Qwen FP8). Point estimates of $100\%$ selective accuracy among served items are finite-sample statements and do not imply zero true error or guaranteed correctness.

AWQ-4 reduces strict-consensus coverage in some configurations, most clearly for Llama-8B: $70.2\%$ versus $76.2\%$ for BF16, a paired difference of $-6.0$\,pp ($95\%$ CI $[-9.4,-2.6]$). Llama AWQ-4 $\ge 4/5$ coverage falls $3.2$\,pp ($[-6.0,-0.6]$). Qwen AWQ-4 $\ge 4/5$ coverage falls $2.2$\,pp ($[-4.0,-0.6]$). The $\ge 3/5$ AWQ contrasts, and all GPTQ-4 coverage contrasts, have paired intervals that include zero. No clear FP8--BF16 difference was detected in modal-agreement coverage or selective risk under these configurations. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:modal-paired} lists the full threshold pattern.

The five-sample token-cost proxy is paid before abstention. Mean $T_5$ is $20{,}057$ / $20{,}038$ / $21{,}327$ / $21{,}436$ tokens for Qwen BF16/FP8/AWQ-4/GPTQ-4 and $23{,}283$ / $22{,}754$ / $23{,}682$ / $24{,}203$ for Llama. Qwen 4-bit retains its approximately $+6$--$7\%$ output-token penalty in this accounting because $T_5$ is the sum of the same five traces. Tokens per served query divide that total by the number served, so abstained items are not free. This is not a measured monetary cost. Figure~\ref{fig:modal} shows the three operating points.

\subsection{Measured serving throughput and Cost-of-Pass}
\label{sec:serving-results}
Table~\ref{tab:serving-batched} reports the batched condition (100 prompts, continuous batching). Qwen-7B FP8 is $+18.7\%$ output tok/s versus BF16 ($824.27\pm 4.00$ vs.\ $694.27\pm 0.65$) and $-19.8\%$ measured $C_{\mathrm{pass}}$ ($\$0.0020$ vs.\ $\$0.0025$) at $\$1.50$/A100-hour. Llama-8B FP8 is $+16.9\%$ tok/s and $-7.4\%$ $C_{\mathrm{pass}}$. These FP8 numbers are A100 Marlin W8A16, not Hopper W8A8.

Four-bit speed is not one number. Batched Llama GPTQ-4 reaches $785.55\pm 12.33$ tok/s ($+20.9\%$ vs.\ Llama BF16). Qwen GPTQ-4 is slower than Qwen BF16 ($-13.2\%$). Llama AWQ-4 batched mean $545.68\pm 125.62$ tok/s includes a same-token, slower third repeat on a different node ($368$ tok/s vs.\ $\sim 634$ tok/s on the other two); we report the protocol $R=3$ mean and do not drop the outlier. Single-stream 4-bit is uniformly slower than matched BF16 (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:serving-single}): Qwen AWQ-4 $-8.9\%$, Qwen GPTQ-4 $-14.5\%$, Llama AWQ-4 $-5.4\%$, Llama GPTQ-4 $-2.5\%$ tok/s. That is Marlin dequant overhead at concurrency 1, not a memory-capacity result.

Token inflation does not automatically cancel a kernel speedup. On the batched subset, Qwen AWQ-4 is $+3.8\%$ mean output tokens vs.\ BF16 and $-1.0\%$ tok/s, so GPU-sec/query still rises $+4.8\%$. Qwen GPTQ-4 is $+3.6\%$ tokens and $-13.2\%$ tok/s. Llama GPTQ-4 is $+15.9\%$ tokens but $+20.9\%$ tok/s (time ratio $0.96$). Cost is tokens $\times$ cost-per-token; neither factor alone is the ranking.

Peak allocated VRAM is $55.2$--$56.7$\,GB for every batched cell. That is the $0.75$ utilization pool, not a measurement of 4-bit weight-footprint savings. Memory and latency are separate.

On (pass@1, measured $C_{\mathrm{pass}}$) over the eight batched points, the nondominated set is $\{$Qwen-7B FP8$\}$. Within Llama the nondominated point is Llama FP8. Qwen GPTQ-4 is dominated. We do not call any cell a ``true Pareto optimum'': the frontier is two-objective, scenario-priced, and stack-specific (Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}). The old $65$ tok/s proxy (Table~\ref{tab:economics}) remains a sensitivity, not the primary cost evidence.

\section{Discussion}
Under this pinned A100 W8A16 stack, FP8-checkpoint serving tracks BF16 pass@1 closely on the evaluated distillations, but we do not establish $\pm 1$\,pp equivalence on MATH-500. Four-bit behavior is not a single story: Qwen keeps MATH-500 and GSM8K near BF16 while losing 5.6\,pp on GPQA-Diamond under AWQ-4; Llama AWQ-4 is the MATH/GSM8K weak cell. Mean 4-bit lengthening on MATH-500 is modest and, when present, lives mostly on traces that also lose the answer. That is a sharper claim than ``4-bit inflates reasoning'' and a more honest one than ``nothing happens.''

Pass@1 and stochastic output stability are related but distinct. Llama AWQ-4's degradation appears in both mean accuracy and 5/5 consensus coverage. Qwen 4-bit formats retain relatively strong MATH-500 pass@1 while spending more tokens. No clear FP8--BF16 difference was detected in modal-agreement coverage or selective risk under these configurations; paired intervals that include zero do not prove equivalence. Repeated sampling sharply reduces selective risk on MATH-500 but costs about five generations per attempted item. The construction is not a general uncertainty estimator and is not a safety property; it is an abstention rule on MATH-500 that requires five generations before the decision.

We previously wrote that pinning vLLM~0.7.0 ``removed'' truncations and loops seen on vLLM~0.8.5. Those earlier runs also differed in harness, decoding flags (including a bug that dropped \texttt{repetition\_penalty}), and prompt profile. The final grid still contains 25 loops. We therefore do not attribute pathology rates causally to the engine version. A clean stack $\times$ format factorial remains future work, not a result in this paper.

FP8 is a reasonable \emph{accuracy} default on this A100 W8A16 path for these two distillations. On the measured batched subset it is also the lowest $C_{\mathrm{pass}}$ point in the eight-cell (pass@1, cost) set, at $+18.7\%$ Qwen tok/s versus BF16. It is still not Hopper native W8A8, and single-stream FP8 does not show the same cost cut (Qwen FP8 $C_{\mathrm{pass}}$ $+1.0\%$ vs.\ BF16 at $C=1$).

Cost is tokens and cost-per-token. Qwen 4-bit lengthening on the 56k MATH-500 grid is real; on the serving subset it does not, by itself, decide dollars. Batched 4-bit kernels can raise tok/s (Llama GPTQ-4 $+20.9\%$) or not (Qwen GPTQ-4 $-13.2\%$). Single-stream 4-bit is slower than BF16 on every cell we timed: dequant overhead dominates when the GPU is not batch-saturated. Peak allocated VRAM does not separate formats under a $0.75$ utilization cap; do not read Table~\ref{tab:serving-batched} as a memory-compression result.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A100 W8A16 fallback.} FP8 numbers do not transfer to Hopper native W8A8.
    \item \textbf{Measured serving scope.} Throughput, latency, GPU-sec/query, and scenario $C_{\mathrm{pass}}$ are measured on a 100-problem MATH-500 subset (20 sequential prompts for Condition~A), not the full 56k campaign. Pass@1 in $C_{\mathrm{pass}}$ is the 40-cell campaign mean. The $\$1.50$/A100-hour rate is a pricing scenario. Condition~B is a 100-prompt \texttt{llm.generate} (continuous batching; \texttt{max\_num\_seqs} not pinned to 8). Condition~A first-20 level counts are $5/7/3/3/2$. Repetitions share sampling seed $20260816$. Llama AWQ-4/GPTQ-4 records mix hostnames. Peak allocated VRAM is the $0.75$ engine pool, not isolated weight footprint. Energy is not measured.
    \item \textbf{Pathology metrology.} The loop detector is identical-word runs $\ge 20$. Phrase-level cycles and \texttt{finish\_reason} were not saved in the original campaign. Near-cap is a proxy.
    \item \textbf{Modal agreement.} Five generations are required per attempted MATH problem. Only MATH-500 has five seeds in this campaign. Mathematical answer equivalence is evaluator- and task-specific (campaign LightEval~0.8.0 / math-verify). High agreement can still be wrong. The signal is not general uncertainty calibration, not softmax calibration, and not a safety property.
    \item \textbf{Checkpoint provenance.} AWQ-4 is a community quantization of unknown calibration set and quantizer revision. GPTQ-4/FP8 are RedHatAI artifacts (appendix).
    \item \textbf{Scope.} Two 7--8B DeepSeek-R1 distillations, English math/science, one pinned serving stack, one prompt. GSM8K/GPQA use three seeds.
    \item \textbf{Source configs vs.\ launcher.} Files in \texttt{configs/models/} are not the QRM \texttt{inference.py} campaign. Effective KV-cache dtype is not stored per cell. Launcher flags such as \texttt{max\_model\_len=32768} are code/launcher-confirmed, not per-job runtime telemetry.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
On a pinned vLLM~0.7.0 eager stack, FP8-checkpoint (W8A16) pass@1 stays close to BF16 for two R1 distillations, while 4-bit effects are model- and task-dependent and are visible to clustered pass@1 tests that maj@5 McNemar misses. Loops are rare but nonzero; near-cap terminations are more common for Qwen 4-bit on MATH-500. Mean token inflation, where it exists, is concentrated on failures. Observable 5-sample agreement on MATH-500 yields low selective error at strict consensus, at the cost of five generations, and Llama AWQ-4 reduces 5/5 coverage relative to BF16. Measured batched serving makes Qwen FP8 both faster ($+18.7\%$ tok/s) and cheaper ($-19.8\%$ $C_{\mathrm{pass}}$) than Qwen BF16 under a $\$1.50$/A100-hour scenario; 4-bit speed depends on concurrency and kernel, and allocated VRAM does not separate formats. The contribution is a stack-pinned measurement of those effects, not a new compressor and not a serving-stack shift.

\section{Modal-agreement details}
\label{app:modal}
The compact recovered artifact has 20{,}000 rows (SHA256 prefix \texttt{23e9ead021111959}; full digest is in the JSON sidecar). It stores extracted prediction representations, campaign \texttt{extractive\_match}, and completion tokens. It does not store chain-of-thought or problem text. Campaign extraction used LightEval~0.8.0; a later MacBook LightEval~0.8.1 install was not used for these numbers. Campaign extractive match was reproduced on 20{,}000/20{,}000 rows before clustering. Equivalence clustering reported 0 symmetry violations, 0 transitivity violations, and 116 non-unique-mode ties among 4{,}000 groups. Bootstrap uses $B=10{,}000$ problem resamples with seed $20260816$. Secondary ECE/Brier scores (confidence $=m_i/5$) are in the JSON report and are not treated as a primary calibration result.

$T_5$ is the five-sample token-cost proxy: the sum of completion tokens over seeds 42--46. Mean / median / P90 $T_5$ are computed over all 500 items. Tokens per served query and tokens per correct served result use that same 500-item $T_5$ total in the numerator, so abstained queries are not free.
