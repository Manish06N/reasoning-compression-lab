## P29 Lian et al. 2026 (CTIR) - verified against text
- Design: vLLM, temperature 0 (greedy), 100 questions per benchmark sampled with seed 42 (AIME25: all 30), one run per model. Weight-only PTQ (AWQ, GPTQ, HQQ) at INT4 and INT3; Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-30B-A3B, Phi-4-reasoning-plus (14B) (Table 6).
- Headline: INT4 mild but measurable; INT3 large; Qwen3-4B INT3 AWQ 276.1% and HQQ 292.1% average CTIR; Qwen3-30B INT3 AWQ 32.8%, HQQ 60.1%. Our paper's sentence "at INT3 they report inflation of hundreds of percent for the smallest model" is correct.
- Correctness-conditioned CTIR (App. D.1, Fig. 6-7) is run on ONE model (Qwen3-30B) only; our wording "the pattern Lian et al. report as evidence of inflation" is right but should say Qwen3-30B. [TODO: make the paper say it]
- Calibration data matters a lot (Fig. 5): Qwen3-4B INT3 GPTQ, Wikitext -> Math-CoT calibration raises accuracy 28% -> 65% and cuts CoT length 30k -> 12k tokens. => CANDIDATE EXPERIMENT K1: calibration-corpus dependence of the 4-bit excess in R1-distill (self-generated reasoning traces vs generic text).
- No seed placebo, no repeated runs, no configuration control. Threat to our C1: low; differentiation clear.

## P32 Patodiya 2026 (cache-induced divergence)
- Greedy, batch size 1, serial requests; with prefix caching DISABLED, repeated runs are bit-identical (0/800 episodes). Enabling prefix caching changes trajectories on 36.2% (16-bit) rising to 75.0% (4-bit Q4_K_M) of agentic episodes; GSM8K single-turn: items flip in both directions, aggregate accuracy unchanged. Quantization amplifies sensitivity to tiny numerical perturbations.
- Relevance: (a) our runs have prefix caching and chunked prefill OFF (verified in engine dumps) - cite as the reason the check matters; (b) supports the claim that a configuration change is a legitimate numerical perturbation under greedy decoding (our G1/X5 design), and predicts that quantized models diverge MORE under a scheduler perturbation than BF16 does => CANDIDATE ANALYSIS Q-placebo: within-arm seed flip/length-variance of quantized vs BF16 (CPU, partly in gen_lenvar). (c) reproducibility note: batch-size-1 greedy is deterministic, but our max_num_seqs 256 vs 64 is not batch-size 1.
- Caveat for citing: single author, preprint, agentic tool use (BFCL), llama.cpp/vLLM 0.11, RTX 4090.

## P30 Lotfi et al. 2026 (overthinking) - read abstract, intro, setup, appendix A/D
- Models: R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B/7B/14B, R1-Distill-Llama-8B, QwQ-32B; GPTQ and AWQ at 3 and 4 bit (g128), FlatQuant W4A4KV4 / W8A8KV8; T=0.6, top-p 0.95; MATH-500, GSM8K, GPQA-D, LiveCodeBench etc. No mention of seeds or repeated runs (searched).
- The 52% (AWQ 3-bit) vs 26% (BF16) overthinking share is for R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B on MATH-500; "overthinking" = a GPT-5 judge (>95% agreement with human labels on a subset) told to select overthinking whenever the GOLD ANSWER APPEARS in the intermediate reasoning but is not the final answer. That is essentially our proxy P-b (BF16 26% in theirs vs 29-37% in ours; no increase at 4-bit in ours). So our C2 null at 4-bit/7-8B does not contradict a 3-bit/1.5B effect; the paper's C2 paragraph should say so. [TODO edit paper]
- Calibration: GPTQ calibrated on MODEL-GENERATED REASONING TRACES (NuminaMath-1.5, 128 x 2048 tokens) "to mitigate domain shift"; AWQ on Pile (128 x 512). Our self-quantized checkpoints use WikiText-2 (P2 GPTQ/AWQ) and Pile validation (E1 AWQ). => Reviewer-visible gap, same as Lian Fig. 5. CANDIDATE EXPERIMENT K1 (reasoning-trace calibration) strengthened.
